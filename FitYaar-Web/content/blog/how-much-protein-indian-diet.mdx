Protein is the one macronutrient most people under-eat — and the one that matters most whether you're building muscle, losing fat, or just trying to feel strong and full. The good news: you don't need imported powders or a "chicken and rice" diet to get enough. A normal Indian thali can get you there.

## How much protein do you actually need?

For active people, the research lands in a simple range: **1.6–2.2 grams of protein per kilogram of body weight per day.**

- **1.6 g/kg** — general health and staying active.
- **2.0 g/kg** — building muscle.
- **2.2 g/kg** — losing fat, where extra protein protects muscle in a calorie deficit.

So a 70 kg person aiming to build muscle needs roughly **140 g/day**. Want your exact number? Use the [protein calculator](/calculators/protein) — it takes two inputs and gives you a target.

## Why protein matters more than the label suggests

Protein does three jobs no other macro does as well:

1. **Repairs and builds muscle** after training.
2. **Keeps you full**, so you eat less without willpower battles — which is why it's the anchor of any fat-loss plan.
3. **Costs more energy to digest** than carbs or fat, giving a small metabolic edge.

If you're training hard but not seeing results, low protein is the most common reason.

## Hitting your target on an Indian diet

Here's where most "fitness advice" falls apart for Indians. You don't need to live on boiled chicken. These everyday foods stack up fast:

| Food | Serving | Protein |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Eggs | 2 large | ~12 g |
| Curd (dahi) | 1 cup | ~11 g |
| Paneer | 100 g | ~18 g |
| Cooked dal | 1 bowl | ~9 g |
| Rajma / chole | 1 bowl | ~13 g |
| Soya chunks | 50 g (dry) | ~26 g |
| Milk | 1 glass | ~8 g |
| Whey (optional) | 1 scoop | ~24 g |

A day of dahi at breakfast, a big bowl of dal and rajma with lunch, paneer or eggs at dinner, and a glass of milk quietly adds up to 100–130 g without any "diet food."

## Timing: spread it out

You'll use protein better by splitting it across **3–4 meals** (roughly 25–40 g each) rather than one huge hit. That's it — no need to obsess over the exact minute after your workout.

## Two myths to drop

- **"Protein damages your kidneys."** For healthy kidneys, high protein is safe. This myth comes from advice meant for people with existing kidney disease.
- **"Vegetarians can't get enough."** Curd, paneer, dal, rajma, chole, soya and milk make it very doable — soya and whey especially close any gap.

## The bottom line

Pick a target from the range above, spread it across your meals, and lean on the desi staples you already eat. Get the number right and the rest of your nutrition gets easier.
